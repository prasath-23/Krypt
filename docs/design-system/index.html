Screens

Krypt locks apps on a phone so that only a trusted second person, the Guardian, can open them, through links shared in any messenger, fully offline. This is its design language: quiet, ink-first and exact. One brand colour that is barely a colour, a serif voice for the few things that matter, hairlines instead of boxes, and space instead of decoration. It is built for Jetpack Compose with Material 3 and Material Icons, with every asset bundled in the APK.

The person behind a lock may be a child, someone in recovery or someone who chose to focus. Every screen is calm, dignified and firm: never punitive, never alarming, and never hinting at a way around the lock.

Brand book

Principles

  • One thing per screen. Each screen has one serif statement and one primary action. Everything else is quieter.
  • Neutral, like the phone. Pure white and Android's #121212 are the backgrounds, so Krypt blends into the system and the apps around it; near-black and near-white ink carry the brand. Colour appears only when it means something: teal for open, burnt orange for time's up, red for errors, green for allowed.
  • Lines, not boxes. Group with 1dp outline-variant hairlines and surface-container-low sections. No shadows except on sheets.
  • Left-aligned and generous. Text starts at the 20dp gutter (space-5). Sections are space-8 apart. Nothing is centred except the PIN dots, the keypad and the countdown.
  • Say what happens next. Every hand-off (share sheet, Settings, opening the app) is announced in words before it happens.

Voice

  • Short, warm and adult. Speak to the person as "you", and to the PIN holder as "your Guardian".
  • Serif statements end with a full stop: "Locked.", "Done for today.", "Done for now.", "Paused.", "Approved." They are the only full stops in titles.
  • Buttons are sentence-case verbs of at most three words: "Request access", "Paste reply", "Approve and send".
  • Use " · " as the only separator in status lines: "Open · 12 min left".
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no blame. Errors say what to do: "That PIN isn't right. 2 tries left."
  • Every string, label and content description lives in strings.xml, with plurals for counts.

Colour: Soft neutral

Backgrounds are #ffffff in light and #121212 in dark (Android's own dark surface), with no tint. Near-black (#1f1f1f) is primary in light; near-white (#e8e8e8) is primary in dark. Every screen, the lock screen included, follows the Appearance setting: System default (the default, matching the phone), Light or Dark (see ThemeSetting). Map the Material roles one to one onto lightColorScheme() / darkColorScheme(). The Krypt roles (locked, open, caution, granted, flash, on-flash) live in a KryptColors CompositionLocal beside it.

  • Use one primary fill per screen: the primary button, plus the things that show "on" (the switch track, filled PIN dots, the selected segment or chip).

Type

Three bundled families (all SIL OFL, in fonts/; put them in res/font/ and never load fonts at run time):

  • Instrument Serif for statements and titles: display-large (48/52) for the one word on a full screen, display-small (34/40) for screen titles, headline (26/32) for sheets and the summary sentence. Always regular weight; use the italic only for the duration inside the summary sentence.
  • Instrument Sans (400-700) for everything you read or tap: title-large, title-medium for app names, body-large / body-medium / body-small, label-large for buttons, label-medium for chips and badges, and eyebrow for the small capitals line above a title (write those strings in capitals).
  • Geist Mono for numbers that tick or must be exact: mono-package, mono-time (4:59), and mono-countdown inside the lockout ring. The keypad digits use the sans.

Font scale up to 200%: never fix a height on anything holding text. Serif statements wrap; full screens scroll with their action area pinned to the bottom.

Space, shape, size

  • 4dp grid. space-5 (20dp) gutter at every width from screen-min (320dp). space-6 between a title block and its content; space-8 between sections; space-12 above the first eyebrow on full screens.
  • Corners are soft, not round: radius-sm (10) for app icons, keys and chips; radius-md (14) for buttons, fields and segments; radius-lg (22) for grouped sections and the lock-screen app tile; radius-xl (28) for sheets. Only dots, the switch, badges and the time-left pill use radius-full.
  • Every control has a touch-target of at least 48dp. Primary and secondary buttons are button-height (52dp); small actions inside grouped cards (never inside Home rows) are button-height-sm (36dp).
  • Icons are icon-md (20dp) in rows and buttons, icon-sm (16dp) in status lines and badges, icon-lg (24dp) for the lock reason.

Motion

  • Quiet and quick: motion-fast (120ms) for presses, selection and the switch; motion-base (220ms) for revealing a field or changing a status. All transitions use motion-easing.
  • Onboarding's Allowed / Not allowed state fades and scales over motion-indicator (280ms; the spec allows 200-500).
  • A wrong PIN shakes the dots once (280ms). While the PIN is checked, the filled dots breathe.
  • The approval flash runs for motion-flash (700ms; the spec requires at least 500).
  • When the system removes animations, everything is instant except the flash, which shows without motion for 500ms.

Iconography and mark

Material Icons, Rounded (Icons.Rounded.*) only, in on-surface or the colour of their meaning. The exact glyphs are in assets/Icons. An icon beside its own word is decorative (contentDescription = null); every other icon gets a label. There are no illustrations and no emoji.

The mark is a keyhole with a cat's ears, and a smaller keyhole set into its leg (assets/Logos). At a glance it is a keyhole, the one way in; look again and a cat is keeping watch over a lock of its own, as cats guarded crypts in old legend. It is one flat silhouette in on-surface, or near-white on a #121212 tile. Never outline it, add a face or whiskers, or put it in a circle. Set "Krypt" beside it in Instrument Serif; at 20dp and below use the optical small mark (krypt-mark-sm.svg, no inner keyhole); the eyebrow "KRYPT" uses it.

Rules from the spec

These stay true whatever the design does.

  • No PIN entry on the locked phone's unlock path. The lock screen offers only Request access (Send again while waiting), Paste reply and Back to home. Back goes to the launcher, never into the app.
  • PINs are digits only and always masked (dots, never digits). They are never kept in saved state and are cleared after use. Lengths: 4-6 at setup, up to 6 on the Krypt Admin gate, 4-8 on the Guardian screen.
  • PIN checks take about 0.3s on purpose: always show the checking state.
  • Lockouts: the 3rd wrong PIN locks for 30s, then 2 min, 10 min, 1 hour. A live countdown shows meanwhile, and input is disabled.
  • Approval confirmation: the flash (at least 500ms), a vibration and a toast naming the app. No buttons, no fields.
  • Onboarding: permission states appear only there. Progress counts only the 2 required permissions, and Start protecting stays disabled until both are allowed.
  • The Home switch shows the lock setting: on = locked. It stays on while an unlock or repeating time is running.
  • The Guardian screen blocks screenshots (FLAG_SECURE).
  • The lock screen's first frame is its window background, which must equal background in the current theme so nothing flashes.

Logo

The Krypt mark is a keyhole whose round top grows a cat's ears, with a smaller keyhole set into its leg. Read fast, it is a keyhole: the one way in. Look again and a cat sits watch over a lock of its own, the old guardian of crypts. The inner keyhole repeats the outer shape: a round top flowing into a stem that flares at the same angle as the leg.

  • krypt-launcher.svg: the 108dp adaptive-icon canvas, a near-white (#e8e8e8) mark on a #121212 tile, inside Android's 66dp safe zone. For Android 13 themed icons, the same silhouette is the monochrome layer.
  • krypt-mark.svg: the mark alone in near-black (#1f1f1f), for light surfaces.
  • krypt-mark-white.svg: the mark alone in near-white (#e8e8e8), for dark surfaces.
  • krypt-mark-sm.svg: the optical small size, one path with no inner keyhole and slightly deeper ear notches, in near-black. Use it at 20dp and below: eyebrows, the footer, and the notification small icon (export it as a white-on-transparent vector drawable; Android tints it in the status bar).

Use: always one flat silhouette in a single colour, with the inner keyhole cut through. Never outline it, add eyes, whiskers or a face, rotate it, or put it in a circle. Keep clear space of at least half its width on every side. Use the full mark at 24dp and up and krypt-mark-sm.svg at 20dp and below. The smallest size is 12dp tall.

There is no wordmark file: set "Krypt" in Instrument Serif beside the mark, or use the eyebrow "KRYPT" with the mark at icon-sm size.

Components

Each card is a live design reference: tap chips, keys and switches. Guidelines follow each card.

ACTIONS

Buttons

Three button weights and one inline size, all with soft radius-md corners: there are no pill buttons in Krypt.

  • Primary (primary fill, on-primary text, button-height, full width on screens): the one action a screen exists for. Request access, Continue, Approve and send, Start protecting. Never two on one screen.
  • Secondary (1dp outline border): a real alternative that leaves Krypt, like "Open settings", with a trailing open_in_new icon so the hand-off is announced before it happens.
  • Tertiary (text only, 48dp target): Paste reply, Back to home, Not now, Keep it locked.
  • Small (button-height-sm, radius-sm, hairline, 48dp touch target): actions inside grouped cards, such as "Allow in settings" on an onboarding row. Never inside Home app rows: those rows stay uniform, and ending access early lives in the row's sheet.

The consumer provides the label (a string resource), an optional leading or trailing icon-sm icon, enabled, and a busy flag. Busy shows a 16dp spinner before a new label ("Checking PIN"), and the button ignores taps while busy.

Rules - Labels are verbs in sentence case, at most three words. - Never fix a button's height; it grows with font scale. - Compose: Button / OutlinedButton / TextButton with shape = RoundedCornerShape(14.dp), contentPadding 20dp horizontal and Modifier.heightIn(min = 52.dp).

PIN

PIN entry

The PIN entry: a row of dots above Krypt's own numeric keypad. It is used on Set Guardian PIN, the Krypt Admin gate and the Guardian's approval screen.

Shows: one 12dp dot per possible digit, filled with primary as digits are typed. Required positions have an outline ring; optional ones (5-6, or 5-8 on the Guardian screen) have a fainter outline-variant ring. Below the dots is one message line. Under that is a 3×4 keypad of borderless keys (key-height, radius-sm, digits in the keypad style): 1-9, 0 and a delete key (backspace).

The consumer provides the digit count, the maximum (6, or 8 on the Guardian screen), enabled, busy, and the error text.

States - Empty, typing, full. - Checking: the filled dots breathe. The PIN check takes about 0.3s on purpose. - Wrong: the dots turn error, shake once, then clear. The message reads "That PIN isn't right. 2 tries left." (plurals). - Locked out: the keypad dims to disabled-content and LockoutCountdown takes the message line. - Not set up: a message only, with no keypad.

Rules - Digits are never drawn, not even for a moment. There is no show-PIN option. - The keypad sits over a hidden field with KeyboardType.NumberPassword tagged PIN_FIELD_TAG, so hardware keyboards and TalkBack still work. TalkBack reads "Guardian PIN, 4 digits entered", never the digits. - Digits live only in memory: never in saved state, wiped after every submit and whenever the screen closes or rotates. - Keys give a light haptic tick, and no sound.

Lockout countdown

After too many wrong PINs, a thin ring counts down until the next try. It replaces the keypad's message line.

Shows: a 120dp ring (2dp outline-variant track, primary arc) emptying as time passes, with the remaining time inside in mono-countdown ("0:28", or "7 min" past 60s). One line under it: "Too many wrong PINs. Try again when the timer ends."

The consumer provides the remaining milliseconds (LockedOut(ms) or lockoutRetryInMs). The ring ticks every second and calls lockoutElapsed() when it reaches zero.

Rules - The keypad and the primary button are disabled until the ring is empty. - The schedule is fixed and shared by the gate and the Guardian screen, and it survives restarts. 1st-2nd wrong PIN: tries left. 3rd: 30s. 4th-5th: 2 min. 6th-7th: 10 min. 8th and later: 1 hour. - Announce the lockout once; don't announce every tick.

ONBOARDING

Permission checklist

Onboarding is one checklist: each permission is a row with its icon, a plain-words title, its live state and, until it is allowed, a button that opens the right Settings page.

Rows (in grouped surface-container-low sections at radius-lg, divided by hairlines): - Required: - See when a locked app opens: Accessibility (accessibility_new). - Show the lock over apps: display over other apps (layers). - Optional: - Keep running in the background: battery optimisation (battery_alert). - Make Krypt harder to remove: Device Admin (admin_panel_settings). - Alerts about new apps: notifications (notifications).

The consumer provides OnboardingUiState.permissions and each step's settings intent. Tapping a row's button sends the user to that Settings page.

State: "Allowed" with check_circle in granted, or "Not allowed yet" (a required row) / "Not allowed" (an optional row) with cancel in on-surface-variant. It is refreshed on resume and changes with a fade + scale over motion-indicator (200-500ms). Its TalkBack label is the same word.

Rules - The button reads "Allow in settings" with an open_in_new icon, which says before the hand-off that Krypt is leaving. - The battery row adds a "Help for Xiaomi, Samsung and others" tertiary button that opens the OEM pages in OemBatteryLinks.kt. - These indicators appear only in onboarding. - Optional rows never block anything and have no Skip; leaving one alone is the skip.

Required progress

Two thin ticks at the top of the onboarding checklist, one per required permission. The optional ones are never counted.

Shows: two 3dp ticks that fill with primary as each required permission is allowed, and the line "1 of 2 required" in body-small. At 2 of 2 the line reads "2 of 2 required · ready" in granted.

At the bottom of the checklist: one primary button, Start protecting. It is disabled until 2 of 2, with "Allow the 2 required items to start." under it.

The consumer provides mandatoryGranted, mandatoryTotal and canFinish. "Start protecting" calls tryFinish().

Rules - Only the 2 required items move the ticks. - The ticks are one progressbar for TalkBack: "1 of 2 required".

HOME

App row

One installed app in Home. Every row has the same shape and height: icon, name, one status line, switch.

Shows: the app icon at app-icon-row masked to radius-sm, the name in title-medium, one StatusLine (Not locked shows "Not locked" in on-surface-variant with no icon, so no row is shorter and lock_open keeps one meaning), and the Material 3 Switch at the end. There are no buttons inside rows; rows are 68dp tall and separated only by hairlines.

GUARDIAN

Guardian approval

The Guardian's approval in two steps, so the keypad is never inside a scrolling page: choose the access, then confirm with the PIN in a sheet.

Step 1 · Choose (a normal scrolling screen, no keypad): eyebrow "ACCESS REQUEST", the app as the display-small title (or its package in mono with "Why only a package name?"), the age line "Sent 1 min ago · expires in 4:00", DurationChips, and the live SummarySentence. A pinned primary Continue opens step 2; it is disabled while a custom value is invalid.

Step 2 · Confirm (a modal bottom sheet over step 1): the SummarySentence fixed at the top, PinEntry (4-8 digits, dots + keypad), and the pinned primary Approve and send. Nothing in the sheet scrolls; it fits a 320×640dp screen. Dismissing the sheet (drag down or back) returns to step 1 with the choice kept and the PIN cleared.

States in the sheet: typing; checking (dots breathe, "Checking…"); wrong PIN (dots shake and clear, "That PIN isn't right. 2 tries left."); lockout (LockoutCountdown replaces the keypad; the sheet stays open). After success the share sheet opens and the screen shows display-large "Sent." with "You can close this screen."

The consumer provides GuardianPinState and calls setKind() / pickPeriod() / editPeriod() / pickMinutes() / editMinutes() / pickDays() / editDays() on step 1 and approve(pin) on step 2. No ViewModel change: step 2 is presentation only.

Live behaviour (as in the card): changing Once / Repeating / For good, the period, a duration chip, the days or a custom value updates the SummarySentence on both steps at once. Repeating reveals the Every chips and the days chips. A period shorter than a day hides the duration chips that don't fit in it ("Instagram, 30 minutes every 2 hours for 7 days."), and one of an hour or less offers 5, 10, 15 and 30 min instead. Custom in Every reveals the Hours field: "1.5" reads "every 90 minutes", and a period outside 5 minutes to 24 hours shows "Choose 5 minutes to 24 hours, like 1.5 or 1:30." For good hides the duration rows ("Instagram, unlocked for good."). An invalid custom value shows its error ("Choose 1 to N minutes.", where N is 1440, or the period's minutes when repeating, e.g. 90), hides the sentence and disables Continue. In the sheet the keypad fills the 8 dots, and Approve and send enables at 4 digits.

Rules - FLAG_SECURE covers the whole activity, the sheet included. - The choice survives wrong PINs, lockouts and dismissing the sheet. - The request still expires 5 minutes after it was made; if it expires while the sheet is open, the sheet closes and step 1 shows the expired error.

Duration chips

How the Guardian chooses what to allow: a segmented control for how often, and chip rows for how often time comes back, how long and how many days.

  • How often (only when the request says the phone supports more than one time): Once / Repeating / For good, a Material 3 SingleChoiceSegmentedButtonRow, with only the options the phone supports (caps=daily, caps=forgood). The selected segment is filled with primary.
  • Repeating is time that comes back every day, or in a shorter period such as every 30 minutes or every 2 hours. Before Amendment 6 it was Daily.
  • Every (Repeating only, and only when the request carries caps=periods): chips for 30 min, 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours, 6 hours, 12 hours and Day, then Custom. Day is the default. Custom reveals an "Hours" field (PERIOD_FIELD_TAG, suffix "hours", decimal keypad) that takes any period from 5 minutes to 24 hours: "1.5", "1,5" and "1:30" are 90 minutes, "0.5" is 30, "5" is 5 hours, rounded to whole minutes. Anything else shows "Choose 5 minutes to 24 hours, like 1.5 or 1:30." Without caps=periods the row isn't shown, and Repeating means a day.
  • Periods follow the clock from midnight: every 2 hours is 00:00-02:00, 02:00-04:00, and so on. When the period doesn't divide a day, the day's last period is shorter and ends at midnight: every 5 hours ends with 20:00-24:00.
  • For good removes the app's lock on the child's phone, as Home's "Remove lock" does. It has no duration, so the rows below are hidden while it is selected.
  • For how long: chips for 15 min, 30 min, 1 hour, 2 hours and Custom. When repeating, only chips shorter than the period are shown, so the time is still a limit. A period of an hour or less uses 5, 10, 15 and 30 min instead: every 30 minutes shows 5, 10 and 15 min, every hour all four. Every 90 minutes shows 15 min, 30 min and 1 hour. A 5-minute period has no shorter chip, so only Custom is shown, and the minutes switch to Custom for the Guardian to type. If the selected chip is no longer shown, the largest one that fits is selected instead. Custom reveals a minutes field (MINUTES_FIELD_TAG) that takes 1 to 1440 minutes, or 1 up to the period when repeating, with the error "Choose 1 to N minutes." ("Choose 1 to 90 minutes." every 90 minutes).
  • For how many days (Repeating only): chips for 3, 7, 14, 30 days and Custom. Custom reveals a days field (1-365, DAYS_FIELD_TAG), with the error "Choose 1 to 365 days." A caution-container note sits under this row:
    • every day: "Today counts as day 1, including time already used. Need more time today? Choose Once."
    • shorter periods: "Periods follow the clock from midnight: 00:00, 01:30, 03:00 and so on. Time already used in this one counts. Need more right now? Choose Once."
    • periods that don't divide a day add a sentence: "Periods follow the clock from midnight: 00:00, 05:00, 10:00 and so on. The last one ends at midnight. Time already used in this one counts. Need more right now? Choose Once."
    • Both list the first three start times, in the phone's 12- or 24-hour style, then "and so on". With three starts or fewer they list them all: "00:00, 08:00 and 16:00".

Chips are 36dp tall with a hairline outline border at radius-sm; the selected chip fills with primary. There are no checkmarks: the fill is the selection, and TalkBack reads "selected".

The consumer provides the AccessForm and calls setKind(), pickPeriod(), editPeriod(), pickMinutes(), editMinutes(), pickDays(), editDays().

Rules - Defaults: Once, 15 min. Repeating defaults to every Day, for 7 days. - An invalid custom value disables Continue on step 1 of the Guardian flow. - The choice is kept through wrong PINs and lockouts.

Summary sentence

The Guardian's choice read back as one serif sentence: live at the end of step 1, then fixed at the top of the PIN sheet, so they know exactly what they are signing.

Shows in the headline style: - Once: "Instagram, 30 minutes, once." - Repeating, every day: "Instagram, 1 hour a day for 7 days." - Repeating, every hour: "Instagram, 15 minutes an hour for 7 days." - Repeating, any other period: "Instagram, 30 minutes every 2 hours for 7 days.", "Instagram, 15 minutes every 90 minutes for 7 days." or "Instagram, 5 minutes every 30 minutes for 7 days." - For good: "Instagram, unlocked for good."

A period in whole hours is said in hours ("every 2 hours"), any other in minutes ("every 90 minutes"). Only the duration (or "unlocked for good") is set in italic. When the app isn't installed on the Guardian's phone, the package name stands in, set in mono.

The consumer provides the current AccessForm and the app label if Krypt can find it on this phone (by package name), else the package.

Rules - It updates live as the choice changes (liveRegion = Polite), and hides while a custom value is invalid. - The same words go into the message the Guardian sends back: "Krypt reply for Chrome: 30 minutes every 2 hours for 7 days."

Toasts

Unlock flash

The approval confirmation: a soft mint field with a ringed check, a short vibration and a toast, then the app opens. There are no buttons and no fields.

Shows: flash covers the translucent window at 94% opacity. A 96dp on-flash ring holds a check_circle; the ring settles from 70% to full size. The whole moment lasts motion-flash (700ms; the spec requires at least 500ms). Alongside it: a 50ms vibration and the TalkBack announcement "Opened by your Guardian". The toast follows.

Outcomes - Once: flash, then "Instagram is open until 14:35". - Repeating, with time left in this period: flash, then "Instagram: 1 hour a day until Sep 28", "Instagram: 15 minutes an hour until Sep 28" or "Instagram: 30 minutes every 2 hours until Sep 28". - Repeating, this period already used up: the toast only, with no flash: "Instagram: daily time is set, but today's is used up", or "Instagram: repeating time is set, but it's used up for now" for a period shorter than a day. - For good: flash, then "Instagram is unlocked for good". - Error: one toast, then the window closes.

The consumer provides the ApprovalOutcome from ApprovalConsumer, played by UnlockSuccessEffect.

Rules - flash is used nowhere else and never sits under text. - With animations removed, show the field without motion for 500ms.

Time-left pill

The countdown over an app in its last 5 minutes: a small dark pill at the top of the screen that says how long is left, "1:59 left". The app works under it as before.

Shows: a pill at the top centre of the screen, 8dp below the status bar, with fully round ends (radius-full). A 16dp icon (icon-sm), then the time in Geist Mono and "left" in Instrument Sans 500, both at 15sp. The pill is #2E2E2E with #F2F2F2 text (the light theme's inverse-surface and inverse-on-surface), and a 1dp white hairline at 20% lifts it off dark apps.

State When Icon Line
Time left The last 5 minutes timer in the dark theme's open (#7CCFB8) 1:59 left
Last 30 s The last 30 seconds hourglass_bottom in the dark theme's caution (#F5A06A) 0:28 left

The time is minutes and seconds, rounded up, so the last second shows 0:01 and 0:00 never shows while the app is open. It ticks every second.

When it shows: only while a locked app with time is on screen (in front, in split screen, in a floating window or in picture-in-picture), and the screen is on and unlocked. With several such apps, it counts down for the one whose time ends first. It goes away when the app leaves the screen, when the screen goes off, when the app is blocked, or when more time arrives, and it comes back with the app. When a period ends first and the next one starts full, it counts on across the end: with 1 minute every 5 minutes, at 08:04:30 it shows 1:30, and the app is blocked at 08:06.

The consumer provides the time left, in milliseconds. TimeLeftWatcher calls TimeLeftDisplay.show(pkg, msLeft) and hide(), and TimeLeftPill draws it. The text is time_left_pill ("%1$s left") around timeLeftText(msLeft).

Rules - Always dark, like a toast, so it reads the same over any app, light or dark. It doesn't follow the Appearance setting. - It never blocks. When the time ends, Krypt's lock screen replaces the app, and the pill goes with it. - It is an accessibility overlay (TYPE_ACCESSIBILITY_OVERLAY), drawn by the accessibility service: it needs no other permission, takes no focus, and lets touches pass through to the app. - One line, with no button and no full stop. - Its window title is "KryptTimeLeft", which the e2e tests find with dumpsys window windows. The service ignores the window event Android sends for it, by its accessibility class name, so it never counts as an app coming to the front, and repeating time goes on being counted under it.
